# Criteria for Influence

Influence in the music industry is often measured through various criteria, reflecting an artist's impact on both their genre and broader cultural landscapes.

## Popularity Metrics

One significant criterion for assessing influence is the popularity of an artist's music, typically quantified through metrics such as streaming numbers, chart positions, and sales figures. In the 2020s, streaming platforms like Spotify and Apple Music have become pivotal in determining an artist's reach and popularity, with charts like the Official Audio Streaming Chart showcasing the most played songs in the UK.[1][2] Popularity scores can correlate strongly with an artist's collaboration history and engagement metrics, revealing how established relationships within the industry can amplify success.[3]

## Genre and Cultural Resonance

Another vital factor is the resonance of an artist's genre within contemporary culture. For example, the resurgence of Latin dance music, particularly artists such as Bad Bunny and Karol G, demonstrates how genre shifts can reflect broader cultural trends and influence societal tastes.[4][5] Moreover, the ability of music to serve as a vehicle for cultural expression allows artists to shape societal values and norms, positioning them as influential figures beyond just musical parameters.[6]

## Artistic Innovation and Activism

Artistic innovation also plays a crucial role in determining influence. Musicians who push creative boundaries or explore new sounds can redefine genres and inspire future generations. Furthermore, many contemporary artists leverage their platforms for activism, using their music and visibility to address pressing social issues like inequality and climate change. This dual role of entertainer and advocate enhances their influence in both the music scene and wider societal contexts.[7]

## Social Engagement and Community Impact

Social engagement is another essential criterion. Artists who actively engage with their communities, whether through public art projects or collaborative initiatives, foster a connection that can elevate their influence. These projects often reflect the values and needs of the community, allowing artists to be seen as leaders and change-makers within their social fabric.[7]

## Technological Adaptation

Lastly, the ability to adapt to technological advancements, such as the integration of artificial intelligence in music discovery and creation, can also impact an artist's influence. As AI tools become more prevalent in shaping music consumption, artists who effectively utilize these technologies may have an edge in reaching wider audiences and sustaining their relevance in an ever-evolving industry.[8]

# Notable Bands and Musicians

## Overview of Influential Artists

The 2020s have seen a remarkable evolution in the music landscape, marked by the emergence of diverse talents across various genres. As listeners enjoy an explosion of originality, several artists have significantly impacted music history and culture during this decade. Notable figures such as Kendrick Lamar, Billie Eilish, and Megan Thee Stallion have not only topped charts but also influenced cultural dialogues surrounding gender and identity in music[9][4].

## Genre Innovations and Collaborations

A notable trend in the 2020s is the blending of genres, particularly within hip-hop and pop music. Collaborations between regional Mexican artists and other genres have amplified their reach, bringing Latin sounds to mainstream audiences[5]. Artists like Bad Bunny and Karol G exemplify this crossover success, enjoying international acclaim while also resonating with U.S. listeners. Additionally, pop punk experienced a revival with artists such as Machine Gun Kelly and Willow Smith, reflecting a nostalgic return to early 2000s sounds[10].

## Female Empowerment in Hip-Hop

The late 2010s and early 2020s heralded a new generation of female hip-hop artists who emphasize themes of empowerment and sexual liberation. Stars such as Cardi B, Doja Cat, and Ice Spice have achieved substantial commercial success, reshaping the landscape of a traditionally male-dominated genre[5][4]. This wave of artists has cultivated a significant market for women in hip-hop, making substantial contributions to the genre's evolution and popularity.

## The Rise of Bedroom Pop

The advent of platforms like TikTok has facilitated the rise of bedroom pop artists, who often explore personal and relatable themes through their music. This genre has become particularly popular for its accessibility, with artists leveraging social media to reach niche audiences and cultivate dedicated fanbases[4]. Sapphic pop, a subgenre focusing on LGBTQ+ themes, has also gained traction, showcasing the growing diversity of voices within the music scene[4].

## Resurgence of Hard Rock and Metal

While hard rock and heavy metal saw a decline in mainstream popularity, bands like Måneskin have revitalized interest in the genres, with their Eurovision win catapulting them to global fame[5]. Additionally, nu metal has witnessed a resurgence, appealing to Generation Z as groups like Deftones and Slipknot gain popularity through viral internet moments[5].

## Cultural Impact and Activism

Contemporary musicians are increasingly using their platforms to address societal issues, from climate change to social justice movements such as Black Lives Matter[7][11]. Artists are not just creators of music but are also engaging in activism, aiming to provoke discussions and inspire change through their work. This dual role underscores the significant cultural impact of musicians in today's society, emphasizing the relationship between art and activism[7][12].

# Trends in the 2020s Music Scene

The 2020s music scene has been characterized by significant shifts driven by nostalgia, technological advancements, and the impact of social media platforms. A notable trend is the increasing demand for older music, with data from 2021 and 2022 indicating that approximately 70% of music demand consisted of songs from the past. This nostalgia-driven phenomenon saw legendary artists like Bob Dylan and Bruce Springsteen capitalizing on the trend by selling their music rights for substantial sums[5].

## Impact of Streaming and Social Media

Streaming services have transformed music consumption, allowing tracks like Mariah Carey's Christmas hit and Wham!'s "Last Christmas" to dominate the charts during the holiday season for consecutive years. As streaming continues to shape the music landscape, platforms like Spotify and TikTok have become central to music discovery and popularity, with TikTok in particular fostering viral trends that can propel songs to fame[5][4][8].

## Emergence of New Genres and Artists

The 2020s also witnessed the rise of various musical genres and new artists. Bedroom pop, a genre that gained popularity through platforms like TikTok, has seen artists writing relatable songs, sometimes exploring themes such as sapphic relationships, leading to the term "sapphic pop" being coined[4]. Meanwhile, genres like hip hop, R&B, pop, and emerging sounds such as breakcore have resurfaced, blending nostalgic elements with modern aesthetics influenced by internet culture and anime[4][13].

## The Role of Artificial Intelligence

Artificial intelligence is playing an increasingly pivotal role in the music industry, with AI music generators simplifying the creative process for both novice and seasoned musicians. This technology is projected to significantly grow the AI in music market, raising concerns about the potential loss of human expression and originality in music production[14][15]. Nevertheless, AI also offers innovative ways to compose music and personalize listening experiences through advanced recommendations and playlist curation on streaming platforms[8].

## Live Music and the Post-Pandemic Revival

Following the challenges posed by the COVID-19 pandemic, the music industry began to recover by mid-2023, achieving record revenues largely driven by streaming subscriptions and the resurgence of live events[4][16]. The rise of live-streaming platforms has also opened new avenues for artist engagement and brand partnerships, reshaping how music is promoted and experienced by audiences[17].
As we move further into the decade, the interplay of nostalgia, technology, and evolving social dynamics continues to define the trends shaping the music landscape of the 2020s.

# Recognition and Awards

The 2020s have seen a remarkable recognition of artists in the music industry, driven by both traditional awards and new forms of accolades that reflect the changing landscape of popular music. As the decade began, the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic significantly affected live performances and award ceremonies, leading to a period of uncertainty for many artists. However, by mid-2023, the industry rebounded, achieving record revenues of $8.4 billion, indicating a resurgence in both streaming and sales activity[4].

## Major Awards

The Grammy Awards remain a prestigious platform for recognizing musical talent. Notably, the 2020 Grammy Awards featured BTS, who made history by becoming the first Korean act to be named the IFPI Global Recording Artist of the Year[18]. This accolade marked a significant milestone, showcasing the global influence of K-pop and its growing integration into the mainstream music scene. Additionally, the Grammys recognized artists like Ed Sheeran, Adele, and Dua Lipa, who have emerged as leading figures in the European music scene during this decade[4].

## Emerging Categories

The British Phonographic Industry (BPI) revitalized the Best British Dance Act category at the Brit Awards, reflecting evolving musical styles and audience preferences. This category was first awarded in 2022 to Becky Hill, who secured the title again in 2023, while Calvin Harris was honored in 2024, indicating a recognition of artists who excel in the dance genre[4].

## Impact of Streaming

As streaming platforms have grown in popularity, new measures of recognition have emerged. Artists are now celebrated not only for their album sales but also for their streaming metrics. For example, a report highlighted the importance of brand partnerships that align with streaming platforms, allowing for greater visibility and support for artists in a rapidly changing market[12][17].
